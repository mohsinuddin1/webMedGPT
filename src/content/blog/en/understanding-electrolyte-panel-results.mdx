When you visit a doctor complaining of unexplained fatigue, muscle cramps, or a racing heart, one of the first tests they will order is a **Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP)** or a specific **Electrolyte Panel**.

Electrolytes are electrically charged minerals in your blood that control everything from your hydration levels to how your heart beats. Even a slight imbalance can cause significant symptoms. 

If you have just received your lab results and are wondering what terms like "Hyponatremia" or "Hypokalemia" mean, here is a breakdown of the four major electrolytes.

## 1. Sodium (Na): The Hydration Manager

Sodium is the primary electrolyte found *outside* your cells. Its main job is to manage blood pressure and determine how much water your body retains or flushes out.

### What Do the Results Mean?
- **High Sodium (Hypernatremia):** Usually means you are severely **dehydrated**. Your body has lost too much water, leaving the blood highly concentrated with sodium. It can cause extreme thirst, confusion, and muscle twitching.
- **Low Sodium (Hyponatremia):** Can occur if you drink too much water (diluting the sodium), or if you are losing sodium through severe vomiting, diarrhea, or certain diuretics (water pills). Symptoms include headaches, nausea, and in severe cases, seizures.

## 2. Potassium (K): The Heart Rhythm Conductor

Potassium lives mainly *inside* your cells. It is absolutely crucial for proper muscle contraction and nerve function—especially in your heart. 

### What Do the Results Mean?
- **High Potassium (Hyperkalemia):** This is a serious condition that can cause dangerous heart arrhythmias. It is most commonly caused by **kidney disease** (since healthy kidneys filter out excess potassium) or blood pressure medications.
- **Low Potassium (Hypokalemia):** Frequently caused by taking diuretics, heavy sweating, or digestive issues. A classic symptom of low potassium is severe **muscle cramps** (especially leg cramps at night) and a fluttering heartbeat.

## 3. Calcium (Ca): The Bone Builder and Muscle Flexor

While 99% of your calcium is stored in your bones and teeth, the 1% circulating in your blood is essential for blood clotting, muscle contractions, and nerve signaling. 

### What Do the Results Mean?
- **High Calcium (Hypercalcemia):** Often linked to an overactive parathyroid gland (hyperparathyroidism). Symptoms are sometimes summarized as "moans, groans, and stones" (fatigue, stomach pain, and kidney stones).
- **Low Calcium (Hypocalcemia):** Can be caused by Vitamin D deficiency or kidney issues. Severe low calcium causes muscle spasms and tingling in the fingers and lips.

## 4. Magnesium (Mg): The Energy and Relaxation Mineral

Magnesium is involved in over 300 biochemical reactions in the body. It helps muscles relax (counteracting calcium, which makes them contract) and is vital for energy production. *Note: Magnesium is not always included in a standard BMP, so your doctor may have to order it specifically.*

### What Do the Results Mean?
- **High Magnesium (Hypermagnesemia):** Rare in people with healthy kidneys, but can occur if you take massive doses of magnesium supplements or antacids.
- **Low Magnesium (Hypomagnesemia):** Extremely common due to poor modern diets, chronic stress, or alcohol consumption. Symptoms include eye twitches, insomnia, anxiety, and unexplained fatigue.

> **Pro Tip:** If your potassium levels are low and won't go up despite taking supplements, you might actually have a **magnesium deficiency**. Magnesium is required for the body to absorb and retain potassium.
