Understanding diabetes and your Hemoglobin A1C (`HbA1c`) levels gives you a reliable three-month window into how effectively your body regulates blood sugar. Whether you were recently told you have prediabetes or already track type 2 diabetes, decoding `%` and `mmol/mol` targets empowers proactive metabolic care.

Unlike fingerstick glucose checks that capture a single moment, `HbA1c` reflects cumulative glucose exposure inside red blood cells. Pairing `HbA1c` trends with daily glucose monitoring and routine kidney or lipid panels helps prevent nerve, eye, and cardiovascular complications years in advance.

## What Is the Hemoglobin A1C (`HbA1c`) Test and How Does It Work?

Hemoglobin A1C measures the percentage of oxygen-carrying hemoglobin proteins in your red blood cells that have become coated with glucose (glycated) over their 8–12 week lifespan. As explained by the [National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK)](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diagnostic-tests/a1c-test), higher average blood glucose produces proportionally higher `HbA1c` readings without requiring fasting.

- **3-Month Memory:** Because red blood cells circulate for roughly 120 days, `HbA1c` smooths out day-to-day meal and stress swings, with the most recent 30 days contributing about 50% of the result.
- **No Fasting Needed:** You can eat and drink normally before an isolated `HbA1c` draw, though fasting is still needed if paired with fasting glucose or certain lipids in a [standard blood test panel](/blog/how-to-read-blood-test-results).
- **Estimated Average Glucose (`eAG`):** Labs often convert `HbA1c` into `eAG` in `mg/dL` or `mmol/L` so your 3-month average matches the scale on home meters.

## What Are Normal, Prediabetes, and Diabetes `HbA1c` Ranges?

Normal `HbA1c` is below `5.7%` (`< 39 mmol/mol`), prediabetes spans `5.7%–6.4%` (`39–46 mmol/mol` in the US, or `42–47 mmol/mol` / `6.0%–6.4%` in [NHS UK/WHO guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/type-2-diabetes/)), and clinical diabetes is diagnosed at `6.5%` (`≥ 48 mmol/mol`) or higher confirmed on repeat testing. Compare NGSP (`%`), IFCC (`mmol/mol`), and `eAG` units below using [American Diabetes Association (ADA) A1C standards](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/a1c).

| Glycemic Status / Target | `HbA1c` (US / NGSP `%`) | `HbA1c` (SI / IFCC `mmol/mol`) | Estimated Average Glucose (`eAG`) | Fasting Plasma Glucose |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Normal** | `< 5.7%` | `< 39 mmol/mol` | `< 117 mg/dL` (`< 6.5 mmol/L`) | `70–99 mg/dL` (`3.9–5.5 mmol/L`) |
| **Prediabetes** | `5.7%–6.4%` | `39–46 mmol/mol` *(UK/EU: 42–47)* | `117–137 mg/dL` (`6.5–7.6 mmol/L`) | `100–125 mg/dL` (`5.6–6.9 mmol/L`) |
| **Diabetes Diagnosis** | `≥ 6.5%` | `≥ 48 mmol/mol` | `≥ 140 mg/dL` (`≥ 7.8 mmol/L`) | `≥ 126 mg/dL` (`≥ 7.0 mmol/L`) |
| **General Adult Treatment Goal** | `< 7.0%` | `< 53 mmol/mol` | `~154 mg/dL` (`8.6 mmol/L`) | `80–130 mg/dL` (`4.4–7.2 mmol/L`) |
| **Elevated Complication Risk** | `≥ 8.0%` | `≥ 64 mmol/mol` | `≥ 183 mg/dL` (`≥ 10.2 mmol/L`) | Often `> 160–180 mg/dL` (`> 8.9–10.0 mmol/L`) |

> **When `HbA1c` Can Mislead:** Conditions that shorten or lengthen red blood cell lifespan—including untreated [iron deficiency anemia](/blog/recognizing-and-treating-iron-deficiency-anemia) (which can falsely elevate `HbA1c`), recent blood loss, advanced kidney disease, pregnancy, or hemoglobin variants—can skew readings. In those cases, clinicians use fructosamine, fasting glucose, or continuous glucose monitor (CGM) metrics.

## What Symptoms Signal High Blood Sugar or Insulin Resistance?

Prediabetes and early type 2 diabetes usually cause zero noticeable symptoms, which is why screening adults at age 35 or earlier with risk factors is essential. When blood glucose stays persistently elevated above `180 mg/dL` (`10.0 mmol/L`), excess sugar spills into urine and triggers classic warning signs listed by [NIH MedlinePlus A1C guidance](https://medlineplus.gov/a1c.html):

- **Increased Thirst & Frequent Urination:** Kidneys draw extra body water to flush out excess glucose, especially overnight.
- **Persistent Fatigue:** Cells cannot pull glucose efficiently from the bloodstream into muscle tissue for fuel.
- **Blurred Vision:** High blood sugar shifts fluid inside the eye lens, temporarily altering focus.
- **Slow-Healing Cuts & Recurrent Infections:** Elevated glucose impairs white blood cell response and skin repair.
- **Tingling or Numbness (`Neuropathy`):** Long-term glycemic stress injures small sensory nerves in feet and hands.
- **Unexplained Weight Loss:** Particularly prominent in type 1 diabetes or severe insulin deficiency when the body breaks down fat and muscle.

## How Can You Lower `HbA1c` Through Diet, Activity, and Medication?

Reducing `HbA1c` safely relies on balancing low-glycemic fiber-rich meals, post-meal physical activity, adequate sleep, and—when lifestyle alone falls short—evidence-based medications like metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, or insulin. Losing 5–7% of body weight in prediabetes lowers progression to type 2 diabetes by over half.

1. **Prioritize Fiber & Protein Before Starches:** Pairing non-starchy vegetables, legumes, lean protein, and healthy fats with whole grains blunts post-meal glucose spikes.
2. **Walk 10–20 Minutes After Meals:** Muscle contraction pulls glucose out of blood through insulin-independent pathways; aim for `150 minutes/week` of moderate aerobic activity plus two strength sessions.
3. **Track Patterns With Fingersticks or a CGM:** Continuous glucose monitors show real-time **Time in Range (`70–180 mg/dL` / `3.9–10.0 mmol/L`)**, helping spot hidden breakfast or stress spikes that `HbA1c` averages out.
4. **Screen Related Cardiovascular & Kidney Markers:** Diabetes management protects small and large vessels, so physicians pair `HbA1c` with a [lipid panel](/blog/understanding-cholesterol-lipid-panel) and [kidney function (`eGFR` + urine albumin) screening](/blog/how-to-read-kidney-function-test-results).
5. **Follow Prescribed Medication Timing:** First-line therapies like metformin reduce hepatic glucose output; newer agents also protect heart and kidney health alongside glycemic lowering.

## When Do Blood Sugar Extremes Require Emergency Medical Care?

Severely high blood glucose can progress to life-threatening **Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)** or **Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS)**, while excessive insulin or sulfonylurea doses can trigger dangerous **severe hypoglycemia** below `54 mg/dL` (`3.0 mmol/L`).

> **Emergency Red Flags:** Seek immediate emergency medical care (call **911** in the US/Canada, **112** in the EU, or **999** in the UK) for suspected DKA/HHS—glucose persistently **> 250–300 mg/dL** (**13.9–16.7 mmol/L**) with uncontrollable nausea/vomiting, deep rapid breathing, fruity-smelling breath, severe abdominal pain, or extreme confusion—or for **severe hypoglycemia** causing seizures, fainting, or inability to swallow safely.

## What Questions Should You Ask Your Doctor About Your `HbA1c`?

Prepared questions help personalize your glycemic target based on age, medications, and other health conditions:

- **"Given my age and health history, is my personal `HbA1c` target `< 7.0%` (`53 mmol/mol`) or a different range?"**
- **"Could anemia, kidney function, or a hemoglobin variant be skewing my `HbA1c` accuracy?"**
- **"Would a Continuous Glucose Monitor (CGM) or structured fingerstick checks help identify mealtime spikes?"**
- **"Should we also check my urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (`uACR`), `eGFR`, and LDL cholesterol?"**
- **"How often should I repeat my `HbA1c`—every 3 months while adjusting therapy or every 6 months if stable?"**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should I get an `HbA1c` test?

Adults meeting stable glycemic targets typically repeat `HbA1c` every **6 months**, whereas anyone recently diagnosed, changing diabetes medications, or above target should retest every **3 months** so clinicians can measure treatment response accurately across a full red blood cell cycle.

### Can prediabetes really be reversed without medication?

Yes. Structured weight loss of **5–7%**, reducing sugary beverages and refined starches, increasing dietary fiber, and exercising at least 150 minutes weekly frequently returns `HbA1c` below `5.7%` (`39 mmol/mol`). Clinicians sometimes add metformin for higher-risk individuals alongside lifestyle changes.

### What is the difference between `HbA1c` and a daily fasting glucose test?

Fasting blood glucose captures your blood sugar at one exact morning moment after an 8-hour fast, making it sensitive to yesterday's dinner, poor sleep, or morning stress. `HbA1c` averages blood glucose exposure continuously over the past 2–3 months, capturing both fasting and post-meal elevations.

### Why did my `HbA1c` rise even though my fasting morning sugar looks normal?

Normal fasting morning glucose paired with an elevated `HbA1c` usually points to sharp post-meal blood sugar spikes (postprandial hyperglycemia) 1–2 hours after eating carbohydrates. Checking post-meal readings or wearing a short-term CGM reveals which meals or snacks are driving up your 3-month average.
