A urinalysis (urine test) is one of the most common and valuable diagnostic tools used by healthcare providers. It provides a window into your overall health, revealing everything from how well you are hydrated to early warning signs of kidney disease, diabetes, or a urinary tract infection (UTI).

Despite being a simple test, a urinalysis report can look incredibly complicated. It is filled with terms like "leukocytes," "ketones," and "nitrites." In this guide, we break down what your urine test results actually mean.

## The 3 Parts of a Urinalysis

When your urine is sent to the lab, it goes through three main types of examination:

1. **Visual Exam:** The lab checks the urine's color and clarity. Normal urine is pale yellow and clear. Cloudy urine can indicate infection, while reddish or brown urine might suggest blood.
2. **Dipstick Test:** A thin plastic stick with chemical strips is dipped into the urine. The strips change color if certain substances are present at abnormal levels.
3. **Microscopic Exam:** If the dipstick test is abnormal, the lab will look at a few drops of urine under a microscope to check for cells, crystals, or bacteria.

## Key Markers on Your Urinalysis Report

Here are the most common substances tested for on a urine dipstick, and what it means if your results come back "positive" or "high."

### Leukocytes (White Blood Cells)
Leukocytes are white blood cells that your immune system uses to fight off infections.

- **Normal Result:** Negative (or a very low number in the microscopic exam).
- **What a Positive Result Means:** If leukocytes or "leukocyte esterase" are present in your urine, it almost always indicates inflammation in the urinary tract or kidneys. The most common cause is a **urinary tract infection (UTI)**. 

### Nitrites
Nitrites are formed when certain types of bacteria turn normal nitrates (found in urine) into nitrites.

- **Normal Result:** Negative.
- **What a Positive Result Means:** A positive nitrite test strongly suggests a bacterial infection, particularly a UTI caused by *E. coli*.
> **Note:** You can have a UTI without a positive nitrite test, because not all bacteria convert nitrates to nitrites. However, if both **Leukocytes** and **Nitrites** are positive, a UTI is highly likely.

### Protein
Your kidneys are supposed to filter waste products out of your blood while keeping large, important molecules—like protein—inside your body. 

- **Normal Result:** Negative or "Trace".
- **What a Positive Result Means:** Finding protein in the urine (proteinuria) is an early warning sign of **kidney disease**. If the kidney's filters are damaged, protein "leaks" into the urine. Temporary spikes can also occur due to heavy exercise, fever, or dehydration, so your doctor will usually want to repeat the test.

### Blood (Erythrocytes / Hematuria)
Finding blood in your urine can be alarming, but it is not always a sign of a severe problem.

- **Normal Result:** Negative.
- **What a Positive Result Means:** Blood can end up in your urine due to a UTI, kidney stones, intense exercise, trauma, or benign prostate enlargement in men. Less commonly, it can indicate kidney disease or bladder cancer. It requires follow-up testing.

### Ketones
Ketones are produced when your body breaks down fat for energy instead of carbohydrates.

- **Normal Result:** Negative.
- **What a Positive Result Means:** Ketones can appear if you are fasting, on a strict low-carb (keto) diet, or if you are severely dehydrated. However, in people with diabetes, very high levels of ketones can be a dangerous sign of **diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)**, a medical emergency.

### Glucose (Sugar)
Normally, there is no glucose in urine because the kidneys reabsorb it back into the bloodstream.

- **Normal Result:** Negative.
- **What a Positive Result Means:** Glucose in the urine (glycosuria) usually indicates that blood sugar levels are extremely high, which is a classic sign of **uncontrolled diabetes**.

## What is a Urine Culture?

If your urinalysis shows signs of an infection (positive leukocytes and nitrites), your doctor may order a **urine culture**. In this test, the lab attempts to grow the bacteria found in your urine in a petri dish. 

A urine culture takes 24 to 48 hours, but it is vital because it identifies the *exact* type of bacteria causing your UTI and determines which antibiotics will be most effective at killing it.

## 🚨 Red Flags: When to Seek Immediate Medical Attention

> **Important:** Seek emergency medical care or call 911 if your abnormal urine tests are accompanied by any of the following severe symptoms:

- **High Fever or Chills**
- **Severe Pain in your Back or Side (Flank Pain)**
- **Nausea and Vomiting**
- **Inability to Urinate**
- **Passing Large Amounts of Blood Clots**

These can be signs of a severe kidney infection (pyelonephritis), a blocking kidney stone, or other acute conditions that require immediate treatment.
